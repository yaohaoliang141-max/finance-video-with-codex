# Production Rules

## Priority order

1. Preserve original video, narration, and subtitles.
2. Never show MG before its matching subtitle.
3. Make MG specifically explain the simultaneous subtitle.
4. Keep subtitles readable.
5. Make animation varied but visually coherent.
6. Add chapters, progress navigation, and chapter openers.

## Timing audit table

Create this table internally before authoring:

| Subtitle start | Subtitle end | Exact subtitle phrase | MG start | MG end | Visual concept | Layout |
|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---|---|

Fail the audit if `MG start < Subtitle start`. Review consecutive subtitles to ensure a lingering MG does not become misleading after the narration changes.

## Subtitle-to-visual mapping

- Amount, percentage, count: animated number plus proportional bar, counter, or scale.
- Increase/decrease: directional line, rising/falling bar, or expanding/contracting area.
- Sequence or causality: flow arrows or a short timeline.
- Assets/liabilities or double entry: two-sided ledger.
- Trust, acceptance, or networks: nodes and connections.
- Distribution or access: ordered translucent bars or branching paths.
- Definition or conclusion: kinetic headline with one supporting visual metaphor.

Do not invent numerical data. If the subtitle contains no measured values, label charts as conceptual or use qualitative forms without fabricated axes.

## Approved visual direction

- Graphite and black-gray base.
- Warm white primary text.
- Orange as the recurring identity accent; mint, muted blue, and risk red may rotate as secondary emphasis.
- Large Chinese display type, compact mono labels, archival/documentary texture.
- Translucent charts with visible source footage underneath.

## Variation checklist

Across adjacent MG clips, vary at least two of: focal position, layout family, accent color, scale, visual grammar, and entrance direction. Do not repeat the same chart family in consecutive clips unless the subtitle is continuing one comparison.
